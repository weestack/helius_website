Tell me what is broken

A paragraph about what hurts is enough to start. I will reply with what I think it actually is, roughly how long it takes, and whether you need me at all — that last answer is sometimes no, and I would rather say it early.

Let’s talk about your infrastructure

Email is the only channel, and it reaches me directly rather than a shared inbox. A rough description beats a polished brief — I would rather read three honest paragraphs than a requirements document that has been through legal.

Worth including

  • What is actually going wrong, in your own words
  • Roughly what you run on — cloud, bare metal, or both
  • Whether this is a one-off project or ongoing help
  • Any hard deadline, and what happens if it slips
  • Whether anyone internally will own this after I hand over

Not needed

  • A formal RFP or scoring matrix
  • NDA before we have established there is a fit
  • Budget disclosed up front — I will quote and you can react
  • A tidy problem statement. Messy is fine, and more informative

What happens next

I read it, and reply with what I think the problem actually is, roughly how long it takes, and whether you need me at all. Sometimes the answer is that a two-hour conversation solves it, or that another consultant fits better — I will say so rather than stretching to fit the work.
